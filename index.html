    Patient Guidance

    When Should You See a Urologist?

    Many urologic concerns are common and treatable. If any of the signs below sound familiar, a confidential consultation can help you understand what is happening and what to do next. This information is educational and not a diagnosis.

    Pain or difficulty urinating

    Burning, straining, or discomfort when you pass urine can point to infection, stones, or prostate concerns.

    Frequent urination

    Needing to go often, sudden urgency, or waking several times at night may signal a bladder or prostate issue.

    Blood or changes in urine

    Pink, red, cloudy, or strong-smelling urine should be checked by a urologist to find the cause.

    Possible kidney stones

    Sharp pain in the back or side that comes in waves is a common sign of a kidney stone moving through the urinary tract.

    Prostate concerns

    A weak stream, trouble starting, or a feeling of incomplete emptying can relate to an enlarged prostate.

    Male reproductive or genital concerns

    Erectile dysfunction, fertility questions, or genital concerns can be discussed privately with a urologist.
